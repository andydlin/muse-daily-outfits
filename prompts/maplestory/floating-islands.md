# MapleStory floating islands

Use this workflow for standalone floating islands. Use [Hero Pairing](hero-pairing.md) for class and boss design fidelity and [Handmade materials](../shared/plush-materials.md) for the miniature finish. The island canvas is square, 1254 × 1254 px, with no cubby, text, or UI.

1. **Gather references.** Confirm the hero, boss form, map, and any proposed skills, summons, or boss attacks. Use the map for environmental motifs and palette. Use the approved island masters for camera and visual scale.
2. **Block out the encounter in Blender first.** Use simple stand-ins for the hero, boss, and any compact summon. Check the platform footprint, front-rim height, visible floor depth, ground contacts or hover height, relative character size, sightlines, and overlap with scenery. Use a shallow, nearly side-on camera. Recent reference targets are about 83% platform width and a central front rim around y=800–815 px. Compare the visible floor and underside with a suitable approved island. Treat these as layout targets and review scene-specific exceptions. A flat reference plate can test screen-space overlap. Physical depth and camera geometry need a 3D blockout.
3. **Review the empty island first.** Present the composition for approval, then generate the environment alone. Compare it with the Blender blueprint and the closest approved islands at equal canvas size. Check width, rim height, visible floor, underside depth, lighting, and open staging space. Fix framing drift before adding characters. When the design is approved, prefer uniform scaling and repositioning for pure framing corrections.
4. **Recheck placement on the corrected island.** Fit the stand-ins again, then check against the actual character references before locking position. Check clearance around heads, wings, props and summons, plausible melee reach or a clear ranged lane, feet or hover shadows, and competition from bright scenery. For Black Heaven, specifically check Lotus against the right turbine and catwalk. Check that the deck extends visibly behind the characters and that floor seams continue naturally toward rear supports. Confirm placement before generating the character pass.
5. **Review characters before effects.** Check pose, grip, scale, silhouette, gaze, character-reference eye style, and material finish. Match the island’s key light and ground or hover shadows. Use Wild Hunter as the texture reference, with material-appropriate finishes for fabrics, stone, scales and painted metal.
6. **Add effects in a focused pass.** Check that the action reads at gallery size and both faces stay clear. Keep the approved clean base, and use separate effect layers where practical for independent revisions. Render closely coupled props with the character when they share orientation, overlap, bounce light, or a hover shadow. Lotus and the ring are one unit. A detached prop such as a floating book can be placed separately. Test the main attack alone, then add one compact summon only if it reads at gallery size without crowding the action. Give a grounded summon its own contact shadow and keep it visually secondary.
7. **Finish after artwork approval.** Apply the final `#F3E6DD` background and check framing, edges, glow continuity, and the square export. Sample multiple clear backdrop areas and openings to verify the exact flat sRGB value when required. Record any intentionally approved gradient as scene-specific. Present one generation or edit per approved pass.

**Framing lock.** Once the Blender camera and empty island are approved, match the same camera angle and vertical island position in every later pass. On the same square canvas, compare the center front-rim y coordinate and the projected floor depth (the screen-space distance from the front rim to the rear playable deck edge) with the approved empty island. Check that there is still floor in front of the characters and a visible continuation behind them. Use the approved floor depth as the target so neither extra floor nor a pinched strip creeps into the render. Recheck after adding characters, props, effects, or the final backdrop, and correct drift before approving the pass.

**Island faces and eyes.** Use the supplied MapleStory class art or approved class-design reference as the source for each hero’s face, including eye shape, size, color, iris detail, brows, and expression. Anime-style eyes are appropriate when they match the class design. Exclude the Jolly face and Jolly face masters from all island renders. Do not use button-style eyes on island heroes. Keep the existing handmade miniature material finish. This island rule takes precedence over the facial identity rules in Hero Pairing and the shared Jolly and material specs.

## Shape, camera, and scenery placement

- **Platform.** Start with a broad rounded deck that projects as a shallow ellipse from the camera. Keep the curved rear edge, continuous playable paving, and a tapered, irregular rocky underside. Fit the entire island and its tallest scenery inside the frame. Measure platform width at the deck edge and check canopy, wing, or prop overhang separately.
- **Camera.** Use the approved shallow, nearly side-on orthographic Blender camera. The current kit starts at 7° downward, with a deck about 83% of the canvas wide, a center front rim around y = 814, and about 52–53 px of projected deck depth on a 1254 px canvas. Treat these as starting points and record any approved scene-specific differences. Measure the front rim and rear playable edge at center. Judge raster camera angle visually, since rear props can hide paving and make otherwise similar camera views look different.
- **Rear scenery.** Place scenery around the far curve of the same deck. Use smaller apparent scale and layered overlap to show depth. Keep scenery bases behind the standing line, the front and middle paving clear, and enough open space behind faces, weapons, and the action lane. Use map-specific motifs. Loose items such as cutlery should rest at the appropriate angle on the deck and stay behind the figures' feet. Give repeated natural forms distinct silhouettes, branch structures, and height.
- **Grounding.** Place stand-ins on the approved empty-island image before generating finished characters. For a grounded encounter, align both figures to the same playable plane and leave a visible strip of paving between their toes and the front trim. Keep soft contact shadows close to the feet. A figure can partially cover a rear prop when both silhouettes remain readable. Check props that cross behind boots for apparent tangencies at gallery size.
- **Correct drift locally.** When only the canvas placement is off, translate or uniformly scale the existing artwork. If a character pass adds a row of paving or moves the front rim, register the foreground paving and fascia to the approved empty island and check the boots and their contact shadows against it. Keep deck depth, platform width, underside depth, and camera locked independently. If the empty island feels low, judge the full composition with stand-ins before moving the island.

## Material review before effects

- Use Wild Hunter as the material benchmark. Check the island first, then the characters. Inspect at full resolution and at gallery size. Compare an edit beside its previous version on equal-size canvases when the difference is hard to see.
- Keep distinct finishes on the island. Exposed stone reads as matte mineral with fine uneven grain, small pits, and soft facet highlights. Keep true glaze on ceramic, restrained highlights on metal, and visible weave, braid, or bark where those materials call for it. If the underside reads as glazed, refine the finish on the rock faces and preserve their shapes, roots, and silhouette.
- Put character texture where it can read at gallery size. Use directional fibers that follow the shape of hair locks, fine short-pile felt on plush clusters, and visible weave and seams on broad cloth panels. Keep finish specific to the material. Metal gauntlets can stay matte, while glass lenses and glazed or polished accents retain their appropriate shine. Preserve face design, expression, pose, footing, and shadows during texture-only edits.
- Lock the clean character and island pass before adding effects. After the effects pass, check that local glow preserves texture on the equipment and paving and that the original contact shadows still read.

## Checks after each pass

- **Compare against the right baselines.** Use the approved empty island for the platform and the last approved composite for the characters and effects. Compare at the same canvas size. Record the platform's left and right edges, center front-rim y, and rear floor edge before adding characters. For each character, record the approximate silhouette bounds and foot or shadow anchor. After a placement edit, check that moving a figure did not also resize it. For a hover edit, check the visible gap from the lowest point to the deck and the alignment of the shadow underneath.
- **Check readability at two scales.** At gallery size, check faces, silhouettes, and the attack lane. At full resolution, inspect grips, eyes, textile grain, and edge halos. Check the hero’s face and eyes against the class-design reference. Keep the Wild Hunter master for material finish.
- **Check shared lighting before effects.** Compare key direction, highlight strength, and shadows across the island and both figures. Correct distracting hotspots or unreadable faces locally. After adding an effect, check that its spill is localized and that it does not wash out faces or nearby silhouettes.
- **Check depth cues and clearance.** Verify that the face and any enclosing apparatus turn the same way. Check clearance against nearby scenery with the final silhouette, including hair, exhaust stacks, wings, or a ring. Judge summon size by readability and hierarchy at gallery size. Use requested dimensions as targets and report meaningful drift.

## Artist prompt

> Create one square 1254 × 1254 standalone floating-island miniature. Use the approved Blender layout and empty island as the framing and environment references. Lock the approved nearly side-on camera angle, platform width, vertical island position, center front-rim y coordinate, and visible deck depth in every pass. Place smaller scenery along the far curve of the deck. Keep paving continuous behind the figures and leave a visible strip in front of their feet. Follow the approved character references for pose, design, and gaze. Match each hero’s face and eyes to the MapleStory class-design reference, including anime-style eyes where appropriate. Do not use the Jolly face or button-style eyes in island artwork. Use the Wild Hunter master for matte mineral stone, visible character fabric weave and directional hair fibers, and material-appropriate hard surfaces. Match the key light and contact or hover shadows. Render attached or enclosing apparatus with its character. Keep the central action readable at gallery size. Change only the elements approved for this pass. Keep the complete island in frame, with no cubby, text, or UI.

## Load the Blender blueprint kit from Project Sources

Look for `Gizmos-Wall-Island-Blueprint-Kit.zip` or `Gizmos-Wall-Island-Blueprint-Kit(1).zip` in Project Sources. Open the archive and extract the matching camera guide, `Gizmos-Wall-Island-Blueprints.blend`, and `Layout-Measurements.json` into the workspace. Open the guide image to inspect it. The PNGs in the ZIP are sufficient when separate copies have not been uploaded.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Melee | Melee | `Camera-Guides/Melee.png` |
| Ranged | Ranged | `Camera-Guides/Ranged.png` |
| Hover | Hover | `Camera-Guides/Hover.png` |
| Mixed | Mixed | `Camera-Guides/Mixed.png` |

Use the named Blender scene as the starting point and its PNG as the baseline view. For example, start Blaster × Pierre from **Melee**. Save encounter-specific changes in a separate Blender file and export its own square camera PNG. Once that encounter layout is approved, use its camera PNG for all subsequent passes. If Blender cannot open the file, use the extracted PNG and measurements for planning and state that direct scene inspection was unavailable. If the archive cannot be accessed or extracted, use an available standalone guide or ask for the matching image.

## Blueprint comparison at every checkpoint

To start, give the encounter and available references, then ask to use this guide. Follow the workflow above from the requested stage. For a new encounter, start with the Blender layout and present the square camera view for approval. Reuse an approved blueprint when one exists. Use the actual camera render as a visual input for each subsequent generation or edit, alongside the last approved artwork for that stage.

Compare the empty island, character placement, effects, and final backdrop directly with the approved Blender camera view at the same canvas size. Use a short table with **Blueprint**, **Current Pass**, and **Difference** for platform width, center front-rim y, and visible floor depth. Compare the camera angle visually when the current pass is raster art and report degrees only when scene geometry supports that measurement. Label estimates. Also check the floor in front of and behind the figures, ground or hover anchors, silhouette clearance, and the action lane as applicable. Flag drift before asking for approval to advance.
