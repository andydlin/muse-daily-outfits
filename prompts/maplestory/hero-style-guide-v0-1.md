**Status:** Approved November 27, 2026  
**Scope:** Playable character artistic development for standalone MapleStory floating islands.

## 1. Artistic identity

Create premium, photorealistic handcrafted miniature collectibles that preserve recognizable MapleStory character identities.

Characters should communicate dimensional craftsmanship, expressive personality, tactile materials and believable miniature construction.

Maintain a distinct artistic identity from cubby mascots. Do not import Jolly anatomy, button eyes, mascot facial styling or coarse yarn hair.

## 2. Reference hierarchy

Each reference governs only its designated artistic or technical properties.

- Character-specific MapleStory artwork is a Character Identity Reference only: recognizable hairstyle, colors, facial features, personality, costume, accessories and equipment. It does not govern anatomical proportions, facial proportions, rendering style or pose by default.
- Independently selected miniature anatomical references and approved character-specific proportion targets govern anatomy. Preserve identity; reconstruct anatomy in our artistic language. Adapt costume and equipment to anatomy, not the reverse.
- Character Texture Master V3 governs material treatment only.
- Island Composition Master V1 governs island composition.
- Boss Style Master V1 governs boss artistic treatment.
- Approved Hero Design reference sheets support component-level artistic development.
- Measured structural composites govern final island arrangement.

Do not generalize reference-specific proportions, poses or silhouettes into universal standards.

## 3. Anatomy and proportions

**Consistent artistic proportions, flexible character anatomy.**

Preferred artistic characteristics:

- Oversized, expressive heads.
- Compact, anatomically coherent bodies.
- Short, functional limbs.
- Readable hands and equipment grips.
- Dimensional footwear.
- Character-specific silhouettes.

Distinguish anatomical proportions, projected posed height and extended silhouettes.

The original Fighter's approximately 1.79-head posed height is a historical reference-specific measurement, not a universal requirement.

Before generation, approve miniature anatomy references and character-specific targets for head share, shoulder breadth, torso and limb proportions. Distinguish posed measurements from standing anatomy and extended silhouettes. Standing hero calibration, body ratios and character scale remain provisional.

## 4. Facial architecture

**Soft in appearance, smooth in material.**

Use smooth matte anime-inspired faces with soft dimensional contours and subtle tonal transitions.

Facial architecture should support character-specific cheek shapes, jawlines, noses, mouths and expressions.

Avoid glossy plastic, excessive surface noise, coarse textile patterns and mascot facial construction.

The approved facial reference sheet provides artistic examples, not mandatory facial geometry.

## 5. Eyes and expression

Use expressive, character-specific anime eyes.

Preferred research directions include:

- Dimensional iris coloration.
- Controlled catchlights.
- Character-specific eye shapes.
- Deliberate eyebrow and lash construction.
- Readable gaze and expression.

Preserve variation across cheerful, determined, calm, serious and other character-appropriate expressions.

Exact eye dimensions, spacing, iris construction and expression parameters remain open research areas.

## 6. Hair design

**Sculpted in form, plush in material.**

Canonical definition approved November 27, 2026:

Soft, dimensional plush-like hair with carefully shaped locks, fine directional textile fibers, gentle surface softness and diffuse highlights. Preserve recognizable hairstyle geometry while creating the tactile appearance of a premium handcrafted miniature.

Use fine, densely packed plush-like microfibers.

Avoid carved resin, glossy plastic, coarse yarn, excessive fuzz and flat felt shapes.

Separate hairstyle geometry from material treatment.

Paladin V3 provides an approved fine-fiber material anchor. F02-D and F02-E4 provide experimental localized refinement references.

Fiber density, strand grouping, texture intensity and cross-hairstyle application remain experimental.

## 7. Material design

**Cohesive craftsmanship, distinct material identities.**

Maintain physically recognizable material differences:

- Fabric: fine, restrained textile weave.
- Embroidery: delicate dimensional stitching.
- Leather: subtle grain and structured construction.
- Metal: satin-matte hard surfaces.
- Fur: short, controlled fibers.
- Machinery: distinct painted and metallic surfaces.
- Magical materials: appropriate luminous or translucent properties.

Do not make every surface plush.

The Character Texture Master V3 retains canonical material authority. Individual material donors support targeted refinement without overriding character identity.

## 8. Costume and equipment

Preserve class-specific costume construction, accessories, signature weapons and recognizable silhouettes.

Verify weapon identity, physical construction, grip and relationship to the intended action before generation.

Distinguish physical equipment from magical effects.

Do not replace distinctive class equipment with generic weapons or decorative effects.

## 9. Character Orientation & Pose

Maintain character-appropriate, readable poses and native grounded, hovering, mounted or mechanical staging.

Future research may investigate viewpoint control, body rotation, head direction, foreshortening and preservation of identity across orientations.

Proposed orientation codes, angles and multi-angle methods remain experimental.

Introduce controlled experiments only when they address a demonstrated production need and receive explicit approval.

## 11. Production and localized refinement

Follow the current Hybrid Assembly V3 workflow and [Hero Generation Prompt](hero-generation-prompt.md). Retrieve the latest prompt before generation; disclose proposed reusable changes, obtain explicit approval, commit and verify before adoption. Record revision and resolved inputs per checkpoint.

Preserve approved foundations, geometry, character assets and artistic checkpoints.

Prefer targeted localized refinement when it provides better control than full-frame regeneration.

Hero Development 01 established promising experimental methods for selective material reassembly, directional sampling and localized curl refinement.

Validate these methods across additional characters before promoting them into universal production requirements.

Obtain explicit approval before every generation, editing step or permanent specification change.

## 12. Independent Character Integration

Retain independent character development and integration as a future research area.

Potential research includes projected scale, perspective, lighting, contact shadows, occlusion and localized assembly.

No proposed integration method is automatically an approved production standard.

Do not initiate additional Blender development or multi-angle generation without demonstrated need and explicit approval.

## 13. Validation and approval

Validate anatomy against approved targets before approving a new foundation or beginning component refinement. Report measured landmarks separately from estimates; preserve failed candidates and seek approval for correction. Evaluate character identity, anatomy, hairstyle, facial expression, equipment, materials, pose and silhouette readability.

For island integration, additionally validate measured placement, scale, grounding or hovering, lighting, clearance and environmental continuity.

Artistic approval remains separate from structural approval.

Preserve source artwork and approved checkpoints. Report limitations, drift, measured values and visual estimates separately.

## 14. Development roadmap

1. Establish artistic identity and reusable components.
2. Expand the visual reference and individual donor library.
3. Develop additional characters to address artistic coverage gaps.
4. Introduce targeted orientation and pose experiments when useful.
5. Investigate independent character integration.
6. Validate repeatability before approving broader production standards.

The roadmap represents research priorities, not mandatory sequential production steps.
