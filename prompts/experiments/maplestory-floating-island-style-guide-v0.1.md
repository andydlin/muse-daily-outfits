## Visual goal

Photorealistic handmade plush miniature battles staged on floating islands inspired by actual MapleStory maps. Use a side-scrolling gameplay composition with dimensional three-quarter characters, both eyes readable where the pose allows, and a shallow view of the floor. Characters, bosses, terrain and effects should feel photographed together as one physical miniature.

## Hero construction and scale

Use the visible cream facial inset, excluding hair, hat, head ornaments and anatomical ears, as the cheek-to-cheek landmark. View angle changes apparent width, so compare similar three-quarter head orientations and cross-check body proportions.

Scale the hero’s head, body, costume, cape and physical equipment coherently. Preserve class-specific weapon proportions and construction. Accommodate weapon reach through staging, grip, pose and encounter spacing.

Hairstyles, hats, bows, long robes and capes may change the total silhouette. Jumping, crouching and lunging may change crown-to-foot height. Maintain recognizable compact plush proportions through these variations.

Faces use fine short velvety cream nap, a subtly fluffier framing border, rounded dark embroidered/button-like eyes with restrained catchlights, rosy cheeks and expressive brows and mouth. Preserve approved class-specific exceptions from the current canonical specifications.

### Required character-scale review — independent from island framing

**Always evaluate; adjust only when needed.** Matching island width, camera or facial inset width does not establish that the hero has comparable apparent scale or overall visual weight. A scene can pass island framing and still fail character-scale review.

1. Compare the actual output side by side with the approved original Ice/Lightning layout and Fighter example at **equal full-canvas display size**, preserving aspect ratio and all margins. Do not crop subjects or independently zoom the scenes to make them look aligned.
2. Evaluate visible cream facial inset width, head/body proportions, torso and limb scale, and the hero's overall silhouette/visual weight. Compare similar head orientations where possible. Report measurements separately from visual judgments and uncertain landmarks.
3. Inspect hats, feathers, bows, hair, capes and physical weapons as a separate silhouette contribution. Retain recognizable costume and equipment proportions; these may extend beyond the body, but must not make the hero unintentionally dominate. Do not impose identical total hero height across different poses/costumes or shrink every elaborate costume automatically.
4. Check the boss and summons independently: boss-to-hero relationship, silhouette clearance, firing space, secondary hierarchy and encounter-specific proportions. **Summons and deployable support machines may be intentionally smaller than their in-game or canonical relative sizes** when that improves readability and preserves **the main hero (including their piloted mech, when applicable) as the primary focal subject**. Do not scale up summons merely for game-accurate proportions. Preserve each summon’s recognizable silhouette, distinguishing details, grounded/hovering behavior, and plausible attack direction; confirm no summon obscures the hero’s face, key pose or combat lane. Review each summon’s scale separately from hero and boss scale.
5. Record this gate as pass, repair or uncertain. Numeric targets support visual comparison; neither a close face-width estimate nor matched island width alone is sufficient to mark it pass.
6. If the island is aligned but the hero is oversized, propose a **hero-only coherent scale change** for approval. Scale the hero's head, body, hair, costume and physical equipment together; anchor grounded feet on the existing combat lane. Preserve island footprint/camera, boss and summons unless explicitly authorized otherwise. Reconstruct newly exposed scenery and adjust immediate contact shadows/attack connections only as necessary.
7. After any local scale edit, recheck actual hero size, anatomy/grips, foot contact and integration, as well as island framing, boss and summon preservation. Generative changes are not guaranteed exact transforms or pixel preservation. State the requested percentage separately from the measured/visually accepted result.

The approximately 6.8% facial-inset width remains a provisional planning anchor, not a universal pass/fail threshold and not a substitute for this gate. No new universal numeric tolerance or total-height rule is established by the Corsair example.

## Camera and floor

### Approved presentation priority — 2D MapleStory stage, spacious combat lane (November 16, 2026)

**Primary artistic direction:** reproduce the recognizable predominantly **side-on, 2D MapleStory side-scrolling gameplay viewpoint** for floating-island battles. This governs staging and perspective, **not** rendering dimensionality: preserve the photorealistic 3D handmade plush treatment of the terrain, heroes, bosses, props and effects, and the dimensional floating underside.

- Keep the camera low and mostly across the horizontal battle plane, with **shallow visible top-surface depth** rather than a broad isometric tabletop. Preserve enough exposed surface for readable grounding, contact shadows, attack directions and equipment; keep most landscape depth and taller scenery behind the battle lane.
- **Shallow floor does not mean cramped stage.** Maintain clear horizontal encounter space between and around subjects. Do not infer correct hero/boss scale from Ice/Lightning, Wind Archer or Corsair simply because their camera presentations match the desired side-scrolling feel. Their character-to-stage occupancy may be too crowded for future use.
- Use the approved original Ice/Lightning layout as **primary camera/layout authority** and preserve its established numeric working targets; retain existing Fighter/master priorities. Wind Archer and Corsair are **supplemental examples of side-view presentation**, not new masters, fixed character-scale targets or automatic approvals of dense staging. The alternative Integrated Temple Fighter comparison does **not** supersede the specifically preferred LEFT Fighter dynamic-composition reference.
- Apply the existing **independent subject-to-stage occupancy and open-combat-space review** even when the camera and island geometry pass. In the pre-generation plan, reserve room for the encounter's required primary hero and boss, full weapon/attack arcs and planned summons; specifically anticipate a **piloted Mechanic mech**, multiple deployables, or **multiple heroes versus one boss** when the user selects those concepts. Evaluate future additions before approving the empty island, not only after populating it.
- Keep subjects readable without allowing heroes, bosses, mechs, wings or summons to swallow the stage. Solve crowding with encounter-specific composition, pose, distribution of props and *approved* relative scaling; avoid uniform shrinking/enlarging, needless platform deepening or camera elevation as default fixes. Secondary deployables may be deliberately smaller under the existing approved rule, while a piloted mech must remain recognizable and the principal Mechanic hero read clearly.
- For future multi-hero scenes, adapt the existing shared-combat-plane and mutual-facing gate to a **coherent group-versus-boss encounter**: allies should visibly engage the boss along the same 2D gameplay plane, with readable separation and coordinated attack trajectories. This is a planning allowance, not permission to generate a multi-hero scene without separate approval.
- Judge the side-scrolling viewpoint, visible floor depth, true usable combat space, subject-to-stage occupancy, dynamic confrontation and island footprint **independently**. Do not introduce an exact camera angle, floor-depth tolerance or universal character size without further evidence and approval.

### Required camera-perspective fidelity gate — before generation and at review

**The primary `MASTER_Island_Layout_Ice_Lightning.png` reference controls the actual viewing geometry.** Compare it against `MASTER_Island_Melee_Fighter.png` at **equal complete-canvas display size**, without cropping or zooming either image. Reproduce their predominantly side-on MapleStory gameplay viewpoint with a restrained three-quarter dimensional view: the camera looks **across** the shallow combat surface, not steeply down onto a display table. Do not infer or invent an exact focal length or camera angle in degrees from pixels.

- **Before generation:** explicitly confirm the master images are available and prioritize visible floor foreshortening, rear-ground horizon/boundary, upright vertical scenery, hero foot contact and front platform lip. Define a shallow, readable floor rather than a broad horizontal deck. Natural side-view visual hierarchy must survive encounter-specific architecture.
- **Separate independent checks:** camera elevation/perspective, visible floor depth, toe-to-front-lip clearance, open usable combat space, island width, ground-contact y-position and underside taper are **different quantities**. A thinner platform, smaller island or upward translation does not by itself repair a too-high camera. Do not mistake higher rear buildings or bottom-tip position for camera angle evidence.
- **Reject high-angle drift:** excessively broad exposed top decking, exaggerated top-down visibility of feet/props, receding tabletop-like paving and scenery spread as if viewed from above are failures even when the island is detached, the output square and the width nominally matches.
- **Review after generation:** compare the actual full V-series result with both approved masters, classifying *camera fidelity* as pass / repair / uncertain before modifying character scale or texture. If perspective differs materially, propose camera/terrain reconstruction or a master-anchored fresh render, not a global resize or simple crop.
- **No loss of combat readability:** retain enough ground for feet, fixed summons and weapons, and room for attack paths, while respecting the narrow visible ground strip and rearward environmental depth of the masters. Do not automatically inherit Fighter's greater exposed floor.

### Platform-fighter stage camera and shared combat plane — mandatory

Use **MapleStory's own 2D side-scrolling gameplay stage** as the primary presentation analogy. A **Super Smash Bros.-style platform-fighter stage** remains a useful secondary analogy for shared fighting lanes and mutual facing, with **Mario and other 2D platformers** as additional secondary comparisons. These games communicate a shared horizontal fighting lane, even when the scene uses dimensional 3D plush characters and a physical floating-island underside. Preserve the actual approved Ice/Lightning layout master as the visual authority; do not copy a particular game's art style, assets or exact rendering projection.

**Mandatory shared-combat-plane / mutual-facing gate:**

- Stage the hero and boss as **opponents on the same side-scrolling combat lane**, clearly **facing and engaging each other**. For a hero at left and boss at right, their head, torso, gaze and attack directions should collectively read as left-facing versus right-facing confrontation rather than both presenting to the camera.
- A restrained three-quarter turn may expose both embroidered eyes and dimensional body details, but **do not let the hero become markedly more front-facing** while the boss appears smaller, behind or deep into the background. Compare each figure's body axis, feet/hover projection, apparent depth, eyeline and attacks, not merely their screen-left/screen-right coordinates.
- For hovering bosses, a higher y-position is allowed, but the **projected gameplay plane stays shared**: match lateral depth, coherent scale and an attack/gaze connection to the hero. Hovering does not automatically justify shifting the boss backward in perspective.
- **Before generation**, declare the hero's and boss's left/right facing directions, shared-depth staging, eyelines and attack trajectories, in addition to the camera and floor-depth checks. Their mutual engagement and equal-plane reading take priority over an attractive individual three-quarter portrait pose.
- **After generation**, inspect the complete canvas against Ice/Lightning and Fighter at equal display size, then independently classify (a) platform-fighter camera, (b) mutual facing, and (c) shared combat plane as pass / repair / uncertain. A correct low camera or island width does not imply that two opponents share a plane.
- Reject a front-facing hero paired with a recessed or disconnected boss as a **composition error**, not a minor camera/style difference. Repair actor orientations/depth or restart from the masters with approval instead of treating perspective as solved through a global resize.

**Fast review questions:** Does this first and foremost read like a MapleStory side-scrolling stage viewed mostly from the side? Do the hero and boss genuinely face each other? Do they occupy the same readable gameplay plane rather than a foreground hero and background boss? Does the island remain a shallow combat stage rather than a tabletop?

The Mechanic vs Lotus V3/V4 tests demonstrated that a correct floating-island silhouette can still fail this gate by looking like an isometric tabletop. These are rejected perspective references, not approved masters.

#### Default finishing sequence

1. Create and approve the artwork, including lighting and effects, before background finishing. Preserve its original source file.
2. Propose the exterior-only finishing pass and obtain explicit approval. Process one image at a time.
3. Use a controlled pixel mask to replace clear exterior background with **#F3E6DD**. Do not regenerate the scene merely to obtain the background color, and do not depend on repeated generative color-matching attempts.
4. Include genuine background openings, such as open arches and exterior gaps between branches. Protect actual rear foliage, architecture, characters, skin, equipment and other similarly colored artwork. Background classification needs image-specific review.
5. Before applying a color mask, inspect an overlay against the actual artwork. Background-like colors may also occur in cream faces, white hats, robes, blonde hair, feathered equipment, book pages and pale effects. Protect these surfaces explicitly; one global color-distance threshold is insufficient. Distinguish true enclosed background openings from pale interior objects. Use only the minimal edge blending needed to avoid halos. Preserve snow, icicles, fine foliage, vines, translucent ice and soft spell glows. Retain gradual effect-to-background transitions rather than flattening luminous or translucent artwork to solid background color.
6. Preserve the canvas dimensions, margins, island footprint, position, hero scale, ground placement and camera. Change only the approved background mask and narrow blending fringe. Keep pixels outside that edit region unchanged.
7. Export as lossless **opaque PNG**. Preserve the original and finished copy separately. Do not introduce JPEG compression in the final delivery.

#### Required checks

- Verify all pixels designated as clear exterior background are exactly RGB **243, 230, 221**. Blended silhouette pixels and intentional translucent/glowing effects are not classified as clear background.
- Compare source and output to confirm unchanged dimensions and zero changed pixels outside the authorized mask and blending fringe. Do not claim complete pixel preservation when a fringe was adjusted.
- Inspect fine leaves, hanging vines, pale snow, thin icicles, enclosed openings and effect boundaries at full size. Look for missing detail, peach/white halos, hard cutout edges or rectangular remnants from protected regions.
- View the finished image on the actual **#F3E6DD** webpage at intended display size. Check that the outer image rectangle disappears into the page.
- If an edge or effect cannot be separated reliably, report it and propose a targeted repair for approval. Do not expand the mask or regenerate the scene silently.

Masked exterior matching to the previous #FDE1D3 target was successfully reviewed on volcanic, nature and snow examples in this chat. #### Optional transparency and lessons from testing

Transparent PNGs remain available for specifically approved overlap, flexible page backgrounds or other needs. They are **not** the default website export. Obtain approval before switching a scene to transparency.

The PNG format is lossless; risk comes from extraction or regeneration, not the file format. Distinguish these methods:

| Method | Preservation considerations |
| --- | --- |

| Non-generative alpha extraction | Can preserve interior RGB, but fine edges, translucent ice and glows require careful alpha and matte handling |
| Generative transparency edit | Can change texture, lighting, shapes, proportions and framing as well as the background; visual acceptance does not establish pixel preservation |

### Choose the repair from the failure

1. **Whole scene too large or displaced, with sound relative proportions:** propose a controlled, non-generative uniform pixel resize and translation of all artwork together and obtain explicit authorization for that method. Prefer it over repeated generative framing edits when preservation of approved detail matters. Never stretch only width to meet a target. Recheck face scale, scenery ceiling, ground line and underside afterward. Do not stretch horizontally/vertically to force all landmarks.
2. **Hero or environment detail scale wrong relative to platform:** whole-image resizing cannot solve it. Propose a targeted subject/environment composition change, preserving approved elements where feasible.
3. **Scenery too tall or underside too deep after framing normalization:** propose an environment-only change; keep hero scale, ground placement and platform width stable.
4. **Grip, anatomy, identity or integration failure:** propose a localized repair. Do not rebuild the whole image solely for a small fix.
5. **Background mismatch only:** use the approved masked pixel finish, not a generative redraw.

Check the complete result again after any repair. Obtain approval before image edits and process one image at a time.
